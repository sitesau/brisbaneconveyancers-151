Conveyancing Services Brisbane Common Mistakes Explained

Conveyancing in Brisbane involves fixed-fee property settlement managed by solicitors who are members of the Queensland Law Society. This process covers everything from the initial contract review through to the final settlement day. It's important for buyers and sellers to have a clear checklist, identifying the nature of the transaction, whether it's a purchase, sale, title transfer, off-the-plan contract, auction purchase, or commercial property. Confirming what is included in the service is vital before accepting a quote.

A key distinction to understand is the difference between the solicitor's professional fee and any third-party costs. Fixed professional fees for routine matters are typically confirmed in writing, while government charges, searches, and transfer duty are itemised separately. This separation allows for a more accurate comparison of costs. Buyers and sellers should inquire about the specific searches expected for the property and any optional ones. Clarification on how transfer duty will be calculated and when it needs to be arranged is also important. Furthermore, understanding who will review any special conditions in the contract is crucial, especially regarding finance or settlement dates that may be difficult to alter.

Contract Review: Preventing Issues Before Settlement

Contract review is a critical step where a solicitor examines the legal standing of a property transaction, going beyond just the final documentation. For buyers, this process includes reviewing the contract, conducting title and property searches, advising on conditions and deadlines, arranging transfer duty, coordinating with the lender and the other party, and managing the settlement to ensure ownership transfer is accurate. The outcome for a buyer should be a clear list of contract dates, conditions, and search-related issues requiring attention before settlement.

For sellers, a thorough review ensures their contract and disclosure position can be confidently presented. If a lender is involved, the solicitor's timeline must align with the bank's requirements for documentation and settlement. Before signing any contract, it's advisable to ask whether the terms require review. After signing, confirm critical dates, search arrangements, duty obligations, and communication with the lender. Leading up to settlement, verify that electronic settlement, fund directions, and transfer documents are prepared.

Queensland Cooling-Off Periods and Auction Rules

In Queensland, most residential property contracts include a 5-business day cooling-off period, which commences once the buyer receives the contract signed by both parties. It is important to note that this cooling-off period does not apply to properties purchased at auction. Terminating a contract during the cooling-off period may incur a penalty of up to 0.25% of the purchase price.

These rules influence the order of tasks in a property transaction. For private treaty purchases, buyers might have a brief window after signing the contract to ask urgent questions. For auction purchases, reviewing the contract before bidding is a safer approach, as buyers do not benefit from the standard cooling-off protection after the auction concludes. The key consideration is whether any special conditions, finance timelines, or search concerns need resolution before the buyer becomes legally bound.

Electronic Settlement and Clear Costings

Settlements are increasingly conducted online via PEXA, a digital platform used for lodging documents and transferring property ownership. Buyers and sellers should expect their solicitor to explain the necessary signatures, fund availability, and how the other parties, the lender, and the electronic workspace interact. Clear written costings are essential, as conveyancing involves both professional services and external charges. A comprehensive quote should detail the fixed professional fee for standard matters and separately list disbursements, searches, registration items, and transfer duty.

For more complex transactions such as commercial properties, off-the-plan purchases, auction acquisitions, or family title transfers, the conveyancing process may involve additional considerations and require specialised advice to ensure all legal requirements are met and potential risks are mitigated.

  1. Understand Your Contract. Before signing, have a solicitor review the contract, especially any special conditions, finance clauses, or settlement dates. For auction purchases, this review is critical before bidding.
  2. Clarify All Costs. Ensure your conveyancer provides a quote that clearly separates their professional fee from third-party costs like searches, government charges, and transfer duty.
  3. Inquire About Searches. Ask your conveyancer which property searches are included and which are optional. Understand what each search covers and its relevance to your purchase.
  4. Confirm Transfer Duty Arrangements. Understand how transfer duty is calculated for your specific situation and when payment is due. Enquire about any potential concessions or grants you may be eligible for.
  5. Prepare for Settlement. Ensure all necessary documents are ready, funds are available, and you understand the electronic settlement process through platforms like PEXA.

Common questions

What is the cooling-off period for property in Queensland? Most residential property contracts in Queensland have a 5-business day cooling-off period starting from when the buyer receives the contract signed by both parties. This period does not apply to auction purchases.

Can I withdraw from a contract during the cooling-off period? Yes, you can withdraw from a contract during the cooling-off period, but there may be a penalty of up to 0.25% of the purchase price.

What is the difference between a professional fee and disbursements in conveyancing? The professional fee covers the solicitor's work in managing the conveyancing process. Disbursements are third-party costs incurred on your behalf, such as government charges, search fees, and transfer duty.
